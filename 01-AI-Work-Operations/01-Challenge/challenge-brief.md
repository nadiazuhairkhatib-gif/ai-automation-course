# U1 Challenge — The Work Is Not the Problem

## Scenario

You are part of the operations team at a small **Training & Innovation Center**.

Every week, the center receives information from different sources:

* Emails
* Training requests
* Meeting notes
* Participant questions
* Report requests
* Spreadsheet updates
* Follow-up messages

The team currently handles most of this work manually.

A typical request may look simple:

> "Please follow up with the participants who missed the last training session, send them the next available session details, and update the attendance sheet."

But behind this request are several hidden steps:

* Find the relevant information
* Understand what the request means
* Identify the people involved
* Check existing records
* Decide what action is needed
* Prepare a response
* Update another system
* Make sure nothing was missed

As the number of requests increases, the team starts experiencing:

* Repeated manual work
* Delayed responses
* Lost information
* Duplicate work
* Inconsistent outputs
* Poor visibility of what has been completed
* Errors when information is incomplete

The center is considering using AI and automation.

But there is a problem:

> **The team does not yet understand the process well enough to automate it safely.**

---

# Your Challenge

You have been asked to redesign the way this type of work is handled.

### Your first rule:

> **Do not choose a tool yet.**

Do not start with ChatGPT.

Do not start with Zapier.

Do not start by asking AI to automate the process.

First, understand the work.

# Part 1 — Observe the Work

Choose one of the following scenarios:

### Scenario A — Meeting Follow-up

A meeting produces decisions, action items, responsible people, and deadlines.

The team needs to turn the meeting notes into a clear action plan.

### Scenario B — Training Request

A participant sends a request asking about available training sessions.

The team needs to understand the request, find the relevant information, respond, and record the interaction.

### Scenario C — Support Request

A user reports a problem and asks for help.

The team needs to understand the problem, determine its category and urgency, assign it to the appropriate person, and follow up.

# Part 2 — Map the Process

For your selected scenario, answer:

### 1. What is the input?

What information enters the process?

Examples:

* Email
* Meeting notes
* Form submission
* User message
* Spreadsheet data

### 2. What happens to the information?

List the steps that happen between receiving the information and completing the work.

### 3. Where does a decision happen?

Identify places where someone must decide:

* What does this mean?
* What category does it belong to?
* What should happen next?
* Who should handle it?
* Does it require human attention?

### 4. What is the output?

What should exist when the process is complete?

Examples:

* Email response
* Task
* Updated spreadsheet
* Assigned request
* Report
* Notification

---

# Part 3 — Find the Pain

Look at the process you created.

Identify:

* repetitive steps
* unnecessary manual work
* places where information can be lost
* places where errors can happen
* places where the same information is entered more than once
* decisions that are simple and repetitive
* decisions that require human judgment

---

# Part 4 — Decide Who Should Handle What

Now classify each important step.

Use:

| Type      | Meaning                                                                                                            |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **AI**    | Useful when the task requires interpretation, extraction, classification, generation, or handling natural language |
| **Rule**  | Useful when the logic is deterministic and predictable                                                             |
| **Human** | Necessary when judgment, responsibility, ambiguity, sensitivity, or approval is involved                           |

For each step, write:

> **AI / Rule / Human — and why?**

Do not simply write "AI because AI is smart."

Explain the reasoning.

---

# Part 5 — Design Before Automating

Create a simple process map.

Use this structure:

```text
INPUT
   ↓
UNDERSTAND
   ↓
STRUCTURE
   ↓
DECIDE
   ↓
AI / RULE / HUMAN
   ↓
ACTION
   ↓
OUTPUT
   ↓
REVIEW
```

You may modify the structure if your process requires it.

The goal is not to make a beautiful diagram.

The goal is to make the process understandable.
